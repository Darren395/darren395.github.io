US Electronics Retail — 2019 Sales Performance Dashboard

An interactive Tableau dashboard analyzing a year of retail transaction data to uncover seasonal trends, top-performing categories, regional revenue distribution, and peak ordering hours, built to support inventory planning, marketing timing, and regional strategy decisions.

Tableau Dashboard Design KPI Design Data Filtering

Key Insights

  • Annual revenue was $34.5M from around 178,000 orders, with an average order value of $193.
  • December had the highest monthly revenue at $4.6M, mainly due to holiday shopping.
  • San Francisco had the highest revenue by city at $8.26M, much higher than the second-highest city.
  • Computers had the highest revenue at $12.2M, even though Cables and Batteries had more units sold.
  • 7 PM was the busiest ordering time, followed by the 11 AM–12 PM period.

Problem: Retail management needs to understand 2019 sales patterns to improve inventory, advertising time, and resource allocation.

Recommendation: Increase stock and customer service staff before October–December because of higher seasonal demand. Focus advertising around 11 AM–12 PM and 6–8 PM, when orders are highest. Consider increasing fulfillment capacity in the San Francisco Bay Area because of its high revenue. Also, focus on selling more Computers and Phones, as they generate more revenue per unit than high-volume products like Cables and Batteries.

Data source: This dataset has real retail transaction records from 2019, with around 186,000 orders from a US electronics retailer. I got this dataset from a public GitHub repository that shares open datasets for data analysis practice. I picked this dataset because it is real transaction data, not made up numbers, and it has enough rows and useful fields, like order date, product, price, and customer address, to build a full business dashboard with trends, rankings, and location based insights.

Approach: The data was cleaned and organized with clear fields for order date, product category, revenue, and city and state. Using this data, I built the dashboard in Tableau, including KPI cards for total revenue, total orders, average order value, and total units sold, a monthly revenue trend, a city and category breakdown, an hourly order pattern, and a top 10 product ranking. I also added filters for month and category so viewers can explore the data interactively.
