Indonesian Crime Data Analysis & Interactive Dashboard

👥 Team Project, My Role: Data Cleaning & Measures/Chart Development

A relational data model built in Excel using a star schema (1 fact table and 4 dimension tables) to analyze crime case data across Indonesia, covering case severity, arrest rates, response time, and case resolution status. The model powers a set of PivotTables, PivotCharts, and an interactive dashboard for exploring crime patterns by region, category, and time period.

KPI scorecard and PivotChart breakdowns, arrest rate by crime type, case distribution by province, average response time, case status by year, and monthly case trend.

Key Insights

  • 670 total cases recorded across 89 regions in Indonesia, with 544 arrests made (an 81% arrest rate).
  • Only 159 cases (23.7%) were marked as fully solved, while the rest remained reported, under investigation, or closed without resolution.
  • 416 cases (62% of all cases) were classified as high severity, and the average police response time was 25.9 days.
  • Fraud, Cybercrime, and Corruption were the top 3 crime categories by case count (67, 66, and 57 cases).
  • DKI Jakarta recorded the highest number of cases (107), well above other provinces.

Problem: Police departments need a clear way to monitor case resolution performance across regions and crime types, so resources like officers and investigation time can be allocated where they are needed most.

Recommendation: Prioritize additional investigative resources for Fraud, Cybercrime, and Corruption cases, since they make up the largest share of reported cases. Investigate why the overall solve rate is low (23.7%) despite a high arrest rate, as this gap suggests many arrests do not lead to case closure. Focus process improvements on high-case-volume regions like DKI Jakarta, and review response time procedures given the 25.9-day average, which may be too slow for high severity cases.

Data model: The workbook is structured as a star schema with one fact table and four dimension tables:

  • Fact_CrimeReport — the central table holding case counts, victim counts, arrest counts, response time, severity level, and case status for each report
  • Dim_Location — province, city, and district details for each region
  • Dim_CrimeType — crime category, description, severity level, and legal consequences
  • Dim_Officer — officer name, rank, department, and years of service
  • Dim_Victim — victim demographics, occupation, and injury severity

My contribution: Within the team, I was responsible for cleaning the raw data before it was loaded into the model, and for building the key DAX measures (case counts, arrest rate, solve rate, average response time) along with several of the PivotCharts used in the dashboard, including the arrest rate breakdown and the monthly case trend chart.

Approach: The four dimension tables were connected to the fact table through relationships in Power Pivot, based on their respective ID fields. DAX measures were then built on top of this model to calculate rates and averages dynamically, which were used to power the PivotTables, PivotCharts, and the conditional-formatted interactive dashboard table.
